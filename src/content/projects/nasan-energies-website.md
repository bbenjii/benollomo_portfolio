So Nasan is a recently founded energy company in Namibia. My dad is one of its first employees, so I've regularly asked about what he's been working on. It's been inspiring and educational tbh.

One thing I noticed was that their website, nasanenergies.com, said "site under construction." Turns out nothing was actually being built lol. So I offered to build it, both to help them out and to farm some website-building XP.

They needed a site that explained the company, its services, goals, and principles, using their branding and images. It also needed contact info, a service station locator, news, and a careers page. And employees had to be able to update it without contacting me all the time.

I researched websites of energy companies in the region, like Vivo Energy, Engen, and Mobil, then started designing. I'd wanted to try Google Stitch, an AI-powered UI design tool, so I used it to generate some initial layouts.

![Screenshot 2026-10-15 at 5.27.42 PM](/case-studies/nasan-energies-website/screenshot-2026-10-15-at-5.27.42-pm.png)

Then came the decision: code it myself or use something like WordPress or Wix? Of course, I chose code (for XP farming purposes, tho it came back to bite me later lol). I went with Next.js, my go-to, and Sanity CMS so employees could edit the content themselves.

![Editing the Nasan station locator in Sanity with a live website preview](/case-studies/nasan-energies-website/sanity-visual-editor.png)
![Managing Nasan news articles in Sanity Studio](/case-studies/nasan-energies-website/sanity-article-editor.png)

I built the site, demoed it to Nasan's marketing team, got their approval, and deployed it. Over the summer, while I was in Namibia, we had a couple of meetings and kept in touch to update the website based on their needs.
